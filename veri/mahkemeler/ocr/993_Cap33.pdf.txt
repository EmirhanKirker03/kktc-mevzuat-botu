A Law TO REGULATE THE RINGING OF BELLS, ETC.
[15th December, 1931.)

1. This Law may be cited as the Bells (Regulation) Law.

2. In this Law—
“beli” means any apparatus usually used as a
means of summoning the public and includes the bell
of any church or school,

3. (1) No person shall sound or ring any bell or cause or
suffer any bell to be sounded or rung without the permission
in writing, under his hand, of the Commissioner. Any
permission so granted may in like manner be revoked by the
Commissioner.

(2) The permission granted by the Commissioner under
the provisions of this section shall state the hours at which,
and the periods for which, any bell may be sounded or rung,
and may provide for the sounding or ringing of any bell in
the event of fire or of any other public emergency.

4. The Commissioner shall from time to time publish
in every town or village a list of bells in such village or
quarter permission to sound or ring which he has granted,
setting forth the hours at which and the periods for which
such bells may be sounded or rung and stating the provision,
if any, which he may have made for the sounding or ringing

of any such bells in the event of fire or other public

5. Any person sounding or ringing any bell or causing or
suffering any bell to be sounded or rung otherwise than in
accordance with the list published under the provisions of
section 4 of this Law shall be guilty of an offence and shall on
summary conviction be liable to imprisonment for any
term not exceeding twelve months or to a fine not exceeding
fifty pounds or to both such punishments.

6. (1) Notwithstanding anything in this Law contained,
the Commissioner may from time to time by order, subject
to such terms and conditions as may be specified therein,
allow the sounding or ringing of the bells of all churches
within his District, and he may at any time revoke such
order in respect of the bells of any or all churches within
such District.

(2) Every order made under subsection (1) hereof shall
be published in the Gazette and thereafter the bell of any
church to which the order relates may, as long as the order
is in force and subject to the terms and conditions specified
therein, be sounded or rung,

(3) Every revocation of an order made under this section
shall be published in the Gazette and shall take effect as
from the date of such publication :

Provided that where such revocation relates to the bells
of any particular church, such revocation shall take effect
as from the date on which it is posted at some conspicuous
place in the town or village in which such church is situated.
