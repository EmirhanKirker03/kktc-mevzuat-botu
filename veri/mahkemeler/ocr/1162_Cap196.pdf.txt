A LAW TO PROHIBIT THE MELTING OR IMPROPER USE OF
CoINs.
[17th September, 1942.]

1. This Law may be cited as the Coinage (Protection) Law.

2. In this Law—

“ coin ” means any gold, silver, nickel or bronze coin,
but does not include gold or silver coins used for the
purposes of ornament as hitherto customary in the
Colony.

3. (1) No person shall melt down, break up or use other-
wise than as currency any coin which is for the time being
current in the Colony or in any other country :

Provided that the Governor may, in his discretion and
subject to such terms and conditions as he may think fit to
impose, grant, either generally or in respect of a particular
person or class ot persons, a licence to melt down, break up
or use otherwise than as currency any coin.

(2) Any person who—
(a) acts in contravention of the provisions’ of sub-
section (1) of this section ; or
(b) acts in contravention of, or fails to comply with,
any of the terms or conditions imposed by a
licence granted under the provisions of the
proviso to subsection (1) of this section ;
shall be guilty of an offence and shall be liable to imprison-
ment for two years or to a fine of one hundred pounds, or to
both such imprisonment and fine, and any articles in respect
of which the offence has been committed shall be forfeited.
