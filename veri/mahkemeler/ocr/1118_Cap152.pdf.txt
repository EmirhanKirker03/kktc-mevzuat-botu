A Law To ENLARGE THE POWERS OF COMMISSIONERS TO
COMPOUND OFFENCES,

[14th August, 1936.]

1. This Law may be cited as the Compounding of Offences
Law.

2. (1) When any person is reasonably suspected of
having committed any of the offences set out in the third
column of the Schedule punishable under any of the Laws
specified in the second column thereof, the Commissioner
of the district in which the offence is alleged to have been
committed may compound the offence by accepting from
such person a payment in money not exceeding the maxi-
mum monetary penalty prescribed for the offence by the
respective Law.

(2) When an offence is compoundable under this section,
an attempt to commit such an offence (when such attempt
is itself an offence) may be compounded in like manner.

(3) Where by any of the Laws specified in the second
column of the Schedule it is provided that upon the con-
viction of the offender for any of the offences set out in
the third column of the Schedule any animals or things
in respect of which the offence has been committed shall
be forfeited, the Commissioner who compounds the offence
may, in addition to any payment in money, require the
offender to forfeit any such animals or things or such
part thereof as the Commissioner may think fit.

3. Where any payment has been made to the Com-
missioner under section 2, the Commissioner shall give to
the person making the payment an acknowledgment under
his hand showing—

(a) the amount paid ;
b) the particulars of the offence compounded ; and

(

(6) the name, surname and place of abode of the
person in respect of whose offence the composi-
tion has been made:

Provided that where the Commissioner has exercised the
power conferred on him by subsection (2) of section 2, the
acknowledgment shall show the nature and approximate
value of the animals or things forfeited.

4. When an offence has been compounded under this
Law and an acknowledgment in respect thereof has been
given by the Commissioner under section 3, no proceedings
shall be taken against the person whose offence has been
so compounded in respect of that offence, and the production
of the acknowledgment before any Court shall be sufficient
evidence that the offence has been compounded and shall
have the effect of an acquittal of the person charged with
the offence.

5. Where an offence has been compounded under this
Law and a payment in respect thereof has been made to
the Commissioner under section 2, the amount so paid
shall form part of the revenue of the Colony :

Provided that, where it is by law prescribed that any
monetary penalty in respect of such offence shall form part
of a particular fund, such amount shall be paid into such
fund.

6. The Governor may, by Order in Council to be published
in the Gazette, make any substitution in, alteration of, or
addition to, the Schedule, and thereupon such Order in
Council and the Schedule shall be read and construed
together :

Provided that no substitution, alteration or addition shall
be made whereby an offence punishable with imprisonment
exceeding six months or with a fine exceeding one hundred
pounds or with both is made compoundable.
