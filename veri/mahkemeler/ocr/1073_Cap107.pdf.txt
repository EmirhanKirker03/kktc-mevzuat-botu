3. No person shall depart from the Colony by sea or
air for the purpose of proceeding to any country outside
the Colony unless such person is in possession—

(a) of a passport, valid for the country to which
such person is proceeding, and .

(b) a passage ticket for a country in respect of which
, such passport is valid :

Provided that the Commissioner may at his discretion
grant a permit in writing to any person to depart from
the Colony without complying with any of the requirements
of this section.

4. Any police officer who has reasonable grounds for
believing that a person is about to depart from the Colony
for the purpose of proceeding to any country outside
the Colony—

(a) may require such person to produce to him any
passport or passage ticket or permit in writing
from the Commissioner in his possession, and

(6) may put to such person such questions as may
be necessary for ascertaining whether such
person intends to depart from the Colony.

5. (1) Any person who—

(a) acts or attempts to act in contravention of the
provisions of section 3; or

(0) aids or abets another person to act or attempt
to act in contravention of the provisions of
section 3; or

(c) refuses to produce to a police officer any passport
or passage ticket or permit in writing from
the Commissioner in his possession after being
required to do so under the provisions of
paragraph (a); of section 4 or

(d) refuses to answer or answers falsely any question
put to him by a police officer under the
provisions of paragraph (0), of section 4

(2) If the Court before which any person is brought
for any contravention of section 3 finds such person guilty
of such contravention, such Court shall, in addition to
the penalty it may consider fit to impose on such person,
order such person to pay to Government by way of
compensation the expenses (if any) incurred by Government
on account of, or in connection with the repatriation
of such person to the Colony.

(3) For the purposes of subsection (2) a certificate
under the hand of the Accountant-General stating that
the amount of expenses specified therein has been incurred
by Government on account of, or in connection with,
the repatriation to the Colony of any person named therein
shall be admissible in evidence and shall be conclusive
evidence that the amount so specified has been expended
by Government.

6. Any police officer may arrest without warrant any
person—
(a) who commits or attempts to commit an offence
against this Law; or

(b) whom he suspects on reasonable grounds of having
committed or of having attempted to commit
an offence against this Law.
